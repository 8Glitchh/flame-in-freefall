Q: What hypotheses motivated the SAME smoke experiment?
A: The SAME experiment was motivated by three primary hypotheses regarding smoke behavior in microgravity ($\mu$g) compared to normal gravity (1g):

1.  **Particle Growth:** In $\mu$g, the absence of buoyancy-induced flows leads to higher vapor concentrations and longer smoke residence times near the source, which promotes nucleation, condensation, and coagulation, resulting in larger smoke particles than those produced in 1g [1].
2.  **Aerosol Characteristics:** Liquid aerosol smokes produced in $\mu$g are hypothesized to exhibit substantially larger droplet sizes, with a corresponding decrease in particle number density for the same mass of smoke [1].
3.  **Detector Response:** The changes in particle size distribution and number density in $\mu$g are expected to affect smoke detector performance, potentially causing some detectors to fail to alarm when exposed to this smoke [1].

Additionally, the investigation was motivated by the understanding that without buoyancy, reduced mixing and heat transfer alter the time-temperature history of combustion vapors and particle trajectories, thereby changing the smoke's composition [2].
