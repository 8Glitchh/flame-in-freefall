# Space agency partner research on fire in space

## JAXA (Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency): FLARE
- FLARE (Flammability Limits at Reduced-g Experiment) is an international project led by JAXA, with NASA and ESA, plus universities in Japan, the USA and France.
- FLARE runs on the Japanese Experiment Module Kibo on the International Space Station, using the same hardware as JAXA's Solid Combustion experiment.
- The goal of FLARE is to relate material flammability limits measured on the ground to the limits in microgravity, so that the lowest oxygen level at which a material can burn in space can be predicted from tests done on Earth.
- FLARE tests spacecraft materials such as PMMA, NOMEX, cotton, filter paper and wire insulation, in opposed and concurrent air flow, at oxygen levels up to about 45 percent.
- FLARE aims to give a new fire safety test method for screening spacecraft materials, to address the shortcomings of NASA's current material flammability test standard NASA-STD-6001B.
- Work connected to FLARE led to a Japanese industrial standard for a high flow oxygen index test, JIS K7201-4, published in 2024.
- Source: JAXA FLARE experiment page https://humans-in-space.jaxa.jp/en/biz-lab/experiment/theme/detail/000807.html
- Source: NASA Glenn FLARE page https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/physical-sciences-program/combustion-science/flammability-limits-at-reduced-g-experiment-flare/

## JAXA: Group Combustion
- The Group Combustion experiment burned clouds of n-decane fuel droplets on Kibo between February and July 2017.
- It studied how a flame spreads from one droplet to the next in microgravity, which helps explain how spray fuels burn in engines.
- Source: JAXA DARTS Kibo experiments archive https://legacy.darts.isas.jaxa.jp/app/kibo-pm-experiments/experiment/combustion/gc

## ESA (European Space Agency)
- ESA supports an international topical team on fire risk in reduced gravity, working with NASA, JAXA and Roscosmos.
- ESA research on spacecraft fire safety uses parabolic flights, which give about 20 to 30 seconds of weightlessness, and plans flame tests in partial gravity like the Moon and Mars.
- ESA is also a partner in the FLARE project.
- Source: ESA article No fire in the sky https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/No_fire_in_the_sky_preventing_an_astronaut_s_worst_nightmare
