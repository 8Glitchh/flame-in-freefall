# Smoke Point in Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) Combustion and Fire Safety Summary

## Executive Summary
The Smoke Point in Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) investigates soot formation and flame characteristics under microgravity conditions. Because soot dominates the heat emitted from flames in both normal and microgravity environments, understanding soot-emitting flames is vital for fire safety and combustion system design [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05]. The investigation successfully completed 250 combustion tests using gas-jet fuel mixtures in microgravity, identifying over 70 smoke points to establish estimates for soot-forming conditions and flame dimensions [a15b5b97fa05].

## Research Overview
The SPICE investigation focuses on gaseous combustion to examine smoke points and soot behavior. 
- **Investigation:** SPICE [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05]
- **Experiment Type:** Gaseous [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05]
- **Fuels Tested:** Propylene, propane, and ethylene in various fuel mixtures [a15b5b97fa05]
- **Fuel Geometry:** Gas-jet [a15b5b97fa05]
- **Gravity Environments:** Microgravity (with comparative data referenced for normal gravity) [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05]
- **Operational Output:** 250 total combustion tests yielding more than 70 identified smoke points [a15b5b97fa05]

## Key Experimental Findings
- **Soot Dominance:** Previous experiments establish that soot dominates the heat emitted from flames in both normal gravity and microgravity fires [77db832a9165] (Confidence: High).
- **Microgravity Testing and Smoke Points:** SPICE completed 250 combustion tests using gas-jet fuel mixtures containing propylene, propane, and ethylene in microgravity [a15b5b97fa05] (Confidence: High).
- **Soot-Forming Conditions:** Researchers successfully identified more than 70 smoke points during these microgravity tests, which yielded direct estimates of soot-forming conditions and flame dimensions [a15b5b97fa05] (Confidence: High).

## Fire Safety Analysis
- **Products and Prevention:** Controlling heat emission and soot formation is critical for preventing the spread of fires [77db832a9165, 77db832a9165]. 
- **Spacecraft Safety Implications:** Understanding soot-emitting flames and the precise conditions under which soot forms advances spacecraft fire safety by enabling more accurate predictions of radiative heat emission and total heat release in microgravity fire scenarios [a15b5b97fa05].

## Gravity and Mission Applicability
- **Microgravity:** The SPICE combustion tests were explicitly conducted in microgravity to evaluate gas-jet flames using propylene, propane, and ethylene fuel mixtures [a15b5b97fa05]. Findings indicate that soot dominates heat emission in microgravity fires [77db832a9165].
- **Earth Applications:** Controlling heat emission and soot formation is critical for preventing fire spread on Earth and for designing efficient combustion systems, such as jet engines, power generation boilers, and furnaces [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05]. Furthermore, the smoke-point phenomenon serves as a classical metric used in terrestrial fire modeling and aids in understanding soot growth and emission by non-buoyant flames in practical combustors [a15b5b97fa05].

## Key Conclusions
The SPICE investigation provides fundamental data on non-buoyant gaseous flames by successfully mapping over 70 smoke points across 250 microgravity combustion tests with propylene, propane, and ethylene [a15b5b97fa05]. Because soot governs radiative heat emission in both normal and microgravity fire environments, these findings enhance predictive capabilities for fire behavior, improving both spacecraft fire safety designs and terrestrial combustion engineering [77db832a9165, a15b5b97fa05].
