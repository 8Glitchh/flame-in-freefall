# Spacecraft Aerosol Measurement Experiment (SAME) Baseline Concept and Fire Safety Summary

### Executive Summary
Microgravity combustion and thermal degradation experiments demonstrate that fire behavior, smoke morphology, and aerosol transport differ significantly in space compared to normal Earth gravity [ad58d9505d4e, 9f5de5639f6a]. These differences necessitate spacecraft-specific fire safety systems, robust particle counters, and multi-sensor approaches [ad58d9505d4e, 70ab02f3740a]. The Spacecraft Aerosol Measurement Experiment (SAME) and related precursor investigations (such as the Comparative Soot Diagnostics experiment) examine material pyrolysis, particulate generation, and detector performance under microgravity conditions to protect crew safety and vehicle integrity on crewed spacecraft.

---

### Research Overview
The research examines the physical and chemical behavior of smoke and pyrolysis products generated by various spacecraft materials in microgravity environments, specifically utilizing the International Space Station (ISS) and the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG) [9f5de5639f6a, 21a448cbf63b, 83580d9d43df]. 

Key experimental hardware and testing parameters include:
* **Materials Evaluated:** Teflon (fluoropolymer), Kapton (polyimide film), silicone rubber, cotton lamp wick (cellulosic surrogate), and dibutyl phthalate (liquid plasticizer/aerosol source) [83580d9d43df].
* **Test Matrix Variables:** Sample materials are subjected to controlled air flows (5 cm/s and 10 cm/s), specific weight loss rates (1 mg/min and 2 mg/min), and varying thermal aging durations (1 minute and 20 minutes) [ed46acf47c9c, a5327c81dc99, 7d21e7291396].
* **Diagnostics and Sampling Assemblies:** Includes the Dynamic Diagnostics Assembly (DDA) housing ISS and STS smoke detectors, the Commercial Diagnostics Enclosure (CDE) housing a TSI DustTrak and a First Alert ionization smoke detector, a P-Trak Ultrafine Particle Counter, a Dynamic Diluter, an Aging Chamber Assembly with a mixing fan, and a Thermal Precipitator Module [a0a98a5885e1, 7ffd40704ead, be86127e58ac, d8cb017ec221, 395b9d6e35ce, 8493c29d882b].

### Key Experimental Findings

#### Combustion Products and Smoke Morphology
* **Particulate Composition:** Different spacecraft materials produce distinct smoke types: hydrocarbon fuels produce soot; overheated plastics yield droplets of recondensed polymer fragments; paper and silicone rubber produce liquid droplets of recondensed pyrolysis products [c9af76c8d3b9].
* **Particle Aggregation:** In microgravity, solid-particulate smokes form longer chains and aggregate into larger particles than those generated under normal Earth gravity [9f5de5639f6a].
* **Detector Visibility Discrepancies:** Precursor experience with the Comparative Soot Diagnostics (CSD) experiment demonstrated that clearly visible smoke streams were not always detected by smoke detectors, whereas invisible smoke streams were readily detected [70ab02f3740a].

#### Aerosol Instrumentation Modifications in Microgravity
* **Condenser Droplet Behavior:** In unadapted instruments like the P-Trak, condensed alcohol droplets risk forming a film that bridges and blocks the nozzle or becomes trapped in an internal circular motion, causing device failure or erroneously high particle counts [a0a98a5885e1].
* **Capillary Wicking Solution:** To counteract pooling in microgravity, 60 grooves (0.006 inches wide) were machined down the length of the P-Trak condenser interior to wick the smoke particle and alcohol mixture back to the reservoir via capillary action [fe48c2de38b1].

#### Material Test Matrix Parameters
The SAME test matrix evaluates five materials across airflow, weight loss, and duration parameters:
* **Teflon:** Tested at 5 cm/s and 10 cm/s airflow, 1–2 mg/min weight loss rates, and 1–20 minute aging durations [ed46acf47c9c, a5327c81dc99, 2833a4d872a9, 7d21e7291396].
* **Kapton:** Tested at 5 cm/s and 10 cm/s airflow, 1–2 mg/min weight loss rates, and 1–20 minute aging durations [222a3b3d726b, 054e11efa206, 6c20884d7f74, 0b6a28d6e445].
* **Silicone Rubber:** Tested at 5 cm/s and 10 cm/s airflow, 1–2 mg/min weight loss rates, and 1–20 minute aging durations [89819d2ce57c, 623b19b2f934, 04854f14f76f, 16af550d6bcb].
* **Lamp Wick (Cellulose):** Tested at 5 cm/s and 10 cm/s airflow, 1–2 mg/min weight loss rates, and 1–20 minute aging durations [315a2b1e5014, b2fd9a411c52, 4264e9d209df, ee43e33cf70a].
* **Dibutyl Phthalate (Liquid):** Tested at 5 cm/s and 10 cm/s airflow, 1–2 mg/min weight loss rates, and 1–20 minute aging durations [d524ca7e5921, 0b21684d7e5e, 86848ee55421, 5673ca603b50].

### Fire Safety Analysis

#### Prevention and System Safety
* **Over-Pressurization Safeguard:** In a power loss event, all system solenoids and mass flow controllers automatically close to prevent over-pressurization of 200 psid pressurized lines [db463c3bb306].
* **Containment and Filtration:** Generated smoke and pyrolysis products are contained within smoke tubes and the aging chamber for 5 minutes before being expelled through HEPA filters into the ISS vent or MSG [a840354b1b79]. The MSG air recirculation system serves as a secondary backup particulate containment system [21a448cbf63b].

#### Detection Considerations
* **Sensor Diversity:** Spacecraft smoke detectors must accommodate diverse particulate types (soot, polymer droplets, recondensed pyrolysis liquids) [c9af76c8d3b9]. Multi-sensor setups (incorporating ISS/STS detectors, DustTrak, P-Trak, and ionization alarms) ensure cross-validation of aerosol signatures [d8cb017ec221].
* **Concentration Regulation:** The Dynamic Diluter utilizes a laminar flow element and differential pressure transducer to regulate diluent flow and prevent equipment saturation, achieving setpoint adjustments in approximately 5 seconds [395b9d6e35ce]. The Aging Chamber's mixing fan ensures homogenous aerosol contents prior to DDA disbursal [be86127e58ac].

#### Products and Toxicity
* **Fluoropolymer Toxicity Window:** Pyrolysis products from Teflon present a JSC Toxicity level 3 for 5 minutes after creation; after this window, particles coalesce and drop to toxicity level 0 [2f629f21b6aa].
* **Post-Flight Analysis:** Sample carousels and Thermal Precipitator Modules are preserved and returned to Earth (via downmassing) to quantify actual mass loss during pyrolysis and evaluate physical/chemical aerosol characteristics [83580d9d43df, 8493c29d882b, cbbef89d4ff3].

#### Operational Safety (Lasers and Radiation)
* **Class 1 Lasers:** Diagnostic devices containing lasers (ISS Smoke Detector, P-Trak, DustTrak) fully enclose optical beams to meet ANSI Z136.1-2000 Class 1 standards with zero crew access [ee320102f645].
* **Radioactive Sources:** STS smoke detectors and First Alert alarms contain 1 microcurie of Americium-241 securely retained within their original housings [62f0234808f7].

---

### Gravity and Mission Applicability
* **Microgravity Environment:** The findings and hardware designs (such as grooved condensers and closed-loop containment) directly apply to microgravity combustion behavior and habitat safety on the ISS [83580d9d43df].
* **Partial-G and Earth Applications:** The provided findings do not explicitly detail lunar or Martian partial-gravity applicability. Earth applications cited include material flammability and pyrolysis quantification studies in controlled chambers, improving early fire detection reliability in enclosed industrial environments, understanding particulate composition from fires, and evaluating thermal degradation of polymers, electrical insulation, and elastomer seals [83580d9d43df, 70ab02f3740a, 8493c29d882b, cbbef89d4ff3].

---

### Research Gaps and Limitations
* **Aerosol Measurement Reliability in Microgravity:** Unmodified aerosol counters (like the P-Trak) risk operational failure via nozzle blockage and optical obscuration from liquid film bridging unless modified with capillary wicking structures [a0a98a5885e1, fe48c2de38b1].
* **Detection Limitations:** Visual smoke presence does not reliably correlate with detector response, as visible smoke streams can evade detection while invisible smoke is detected [70ab02f3740a].

---

### Key Conclusions
1. Microgravity alters smoke particle aggregation (forming longer chains) and changes combustion behavior compared to Earth, requiring specialized spacecraft fire safety approaches [ad58d9505d4e, 9f5de5639f6a].
2. Standard aerosol and particle counting instruments require mechanical adaptations (such as wicking grooves) to prevent fluid pooling failures in microgravity [a0a98a5885e1, fe48c2de38b1].
3. Diverse materials generate distinct particulate types and toxicological profiles (e.g., transient JSC Toxicity level 3 for Teflon pyrolysis), mandating robust multi-sensor detection networks and filtered containment systems [c9af76c8d3b9, 2f629f21b6aa, a840354b1b79].
