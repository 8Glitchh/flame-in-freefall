# Dust and Aerosol Measurement Feasibility Test (DAFT) Summary

## Executive Summary
The Dust and Aerosol Measurement Feasibility Test (DAFT) investigation evaluated particulate and smoke characteristics aboard the International Space Station (ISS) using the P-Trak Ultrafine Particle Counter [aef168cb79c8, 4cfdddc880f9]. Key findings indicate that smoke and average particulate sizes formed in microgravity are larger than those observed on Earth [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746], and that ambient particulate levels on the ISS are low relative to Space Shuttles due to filtration and smaller crew sizes [4cfdddc880f9]. Operational constraints were also identified, notably sensor sensitivity to cabin alcohol content [318c0c6a0afd]. These results directly inform spacecraft fire detection, ambient air quality monitoring, and sensor calibration.

---

## Research Overview
The DAFT investigation focused on characterizing aerosol, dust, and smoke environments in microgravity. Measurements were performed using the P-Trak Ultrafine Particle Counter to capture ambient particulate baselines and compare orbital characteristics against terrestrial conditions [aef168cb79c8, 4cfdddc880f9, 9c8062979746]. 

---

## Key Experimental Findings

### Products and Combustion Byproducts
* **Particle Size in Microgravity:** Smoke particles and average particle sizes generated or observed on-orbit are larger than those formed under terrestrial gravity [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746]. This size difference may be attributed to the inefficiency of particle coagulation and growth behaviors in space [9c8062979746].
* **Ambient Particulate Levels:** DAFT data demonstrated that particulate levels aboard the ISS are low relative to historical Space Shuttle measurements, a condition attributed to the ISS’s smaller crew size and active filtration systems [4cfdddc880f9].

### Operational Limitations and Instrumentation
* **Atmospheric Interference:** The P-Trak sensor exhibited sensitivity to the ambient alcohol content of the ISS atmosphere, which restricts operational usage to a maximum of three test points per day [318c0c6a0afd].

---

## Fire Safety Analysis

### Detection and Prevention
* **Sensor Calibration:** Because microgravity smoke particles are larger than terrestrial counterparts, spacecraft smoke and particulate detectors must be calibrated specifically for microgravity conditions to ensure timely and reliable fire detection during incipient fires [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746].
* **Baseline Monitoring:** Establishing ambient particulate baselines aids in setting accurate thresholds for air quality monitoring and anomaly/fire detection systems [4cfdddc880f9].
* **Operational Procedures:** Procedural limits on sensor utilization (such as restricting P-Trak tests to three per day due to alcohol vapor interference) are required to avoid instrument saturation and false readings [318c0c6a0afd].

## Gravity and Mission Applicability
* **Microgravity Environment:** Findings regarding larger smoke and particulate sizes [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746] and low baseline particulate levels [4cfdddc880f9] directly apply to crewed spacecraft operating in microgravity (such as the ISS).

## Earth Applications
* Results comparing on-orbit particle sizes to terrestrial measurements help correlate particle coagulation and growth behaviors between spaceflight and terrestrial environments [9c8062979746].

## Research Gaps and Limitations
* The investigation explicitly notes that larger average particle sizes on orbit may be linked to the inefficiency of particle coagulation and growth mechanisms, pointing to an area for further study [9c8062979746].
* Sensor operational capacity is explicitly limited by environmental sensitivities (ambient alcohol content) [318c0c6a0afd].

## Key Conclusions
* Smoke and average particulate sizes are larger in microgravity compared to Earth [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746].
* ISS particulate levels remain low compared to Space Shuttles due to filtration and crew size [4cfdddc880f9].
* Spacecraft particulate and smoke detection system designs and operational protocols must account for microgravity aerosol characteristics and cabin chemical interferences [aef168cb79c8, 9c8062979746, 318c0c6a0afd].
