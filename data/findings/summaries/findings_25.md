# Burning and Suppression of Solids (BASS, BASS-II, and BASS-M) Research Summary

## Executive Summary
The Burning and Suppression of Solids (BASS), BASS-II, and BASS-M investigations examine solid material flammability, combustion dynamics, and extinction behavior in microgravity environments aboard the International Space Station (ISS) and through drop-tower testing [978285f71094, 17cc5857def6, 901e1f49b24e]. The primary hypothesis tested by BASS is that materials with adequate ventilation burn as well as—or better than—they do in normal terrestrial gravity under identical conditions [978285f71094]. By utilizing modified Smoke Point In Co-flow Experiment (SPICE) hardware within the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG), researchers evaluate varied fuel geometries (thin films, sheets, rods, spheres, and textiles) across controlled forced-convective airflow conditions (5 to 50 cm/s) and inert suppression inputs [07d948d2ca5f, 17cc5857def6, 2cca5c5caaad, 901e1f49b24e]. The resulting data support improved spacecraft material selection, validation of combustion computer models, and the optimization of fire suppression strategies [f451aca5e9ab, 3bb14d0c7f4d].

---

## Research Overview
The BASS investigation series tests solid combustible materials to characterize fundamental combustion mechanisms in space:
- **BASS / BASS-II:** Examines acrylic films, acrylic sheets, cotton-fiberglass fabric blends (SIBAL fuel), black or clear acrylic rods, and large solid spherical acrylic sections [17cc5857def6, bd95587ad68c, be220a548c2c]. Operations involve 41 test samples yielding 89 test points across adjustable airflows (5 to 50 cm/s) and nitrogen vitiation/suppression rates (up to 500 cc/min) [2cb03747edf1, 07d948d2ca5f, bcb125342fcf, 2cca5c5caaad].
- **BASS-M (Milliken):** Evaluates 44 textile samples across 11 material types, featuring integrated hot-wire igniters where an untreated control sample flame impinges directly onto an adjacent treated textile sample [901e1f49b24e, 5873d52278ee].
- **Experimental Hardware:** Uses modified SPICE hardware integrated into the ISS Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG), equipped with diagnostic capabilities including video cameras, still cameras, anemometers, radiometers, and mass flow controllers [c140d99f57a3, 2cb03747edf1, bcb125342fcf, 17cc5857def6].

## Key Experimental Findings

### Combustion Appearance and Dynamics
- **Flame Shape:** In microgravity, fuels burn as symmetric "oval balls" rather than producing the upward-pointed cone flames characteristic of normal 1-g environments [3bb14d0c7f4d].
- **Spread and Oscillations:** Important experimental metrics include flame shape and appearance as functions of ambient oxygen concentration and flow speed, flame spread rates, and combustion dynamics such as pulsations and oscillations [c140d99f57a3, 5043e1b37c99].
- **Gravity Transitions:** Drop-tower tests and computer simulations successfully capture the transient flame response of a 2-cm-diameter PMMA sphere burning in 17% oxygen at 1 atm and 2 cm/s forced convective flow as it transitions from 1-g to 0-g [66b8f4f3d4d3]. A zero-g facility test similarly tracked a 2-cm PMMA sphere in a 30 cm/s airflow from 1-g through 0-g drop to nitrogen agent application [708a8734f9a2].

### Fuel Geometries and Spread Rates
- **Thin vs. Thick Fuels:** Thin flat samples (10 cm long by 1 and 2 cm wide) yield concurrent or opposed-flow spread rates, limiting flame lengths, and extinction limits [de44677adb25]. Large solid spherical acrylic sections enable the study of thick material ignition and flame growth [be220a548c2c]. Rod samples (black and clear acrylic) provide solid fuel regression rates and extinction limits for opposed and concurrent flows [bd95587ad68c].
- **Transition Formulas:** Microgravity data enabled scientists to determine thin and thick fuel spread rates and derive a mathematical formula for the transition from thin to thick fuels [0cc8af50a6e0].

### Extinction and Suppression Behavior
- **Nitrogen Suppressant Application:** In BASS-II operations, samples burn for approximately one minute under controlled airflow (5 to 50 cm/s), after which station $GN_2$ suppressant is introduced via a mass flow controller (up to 500 cc/min) at gradually increasing levels until flame extinction is achieved [9e631276e275, bcb125342fcf, 2cca5c5caaad].
- **Critical Extinction Data:** Key observations governing extinction include time to extinction as a function of fuel geometry, nitrogen flow rate/vitiation, flame distance from the nozzle, flow speed, and ambient oxygen concentration [3d2e21d44b29, d22ae0249f38, 2cca5c5caaad].
- **Textile Self-Extinction:** BASS-M tests evaluate whether treated textiles successfully self-extinguish when subjected to direct flame impingement from an adjacent untreated burning sample under forced convection in microgravity [5873d52278ee, c1235c72b2a0, 0318806a748a].

## Fire Safety Analysis
- **Suppression Challenges in Micro-g:** Attacking the base of a fire as practiced on Earth may be ineffective in microgravity. Depending on flame geometry, extinguisher distance, and dispersion angle, suppressant streams can fail or actively worsen a fire through undesired oxygen entrainment [56b4a5b5e9f0].
- **Material Flammability Screening:** Validating whether ground-based material screening accurately reflects microgravity behavior is critical to ensuring that treated spacecraft clothing, structural polymers, and textiles will self-extinguish and prevent catastrophic fire propagation [f451aca5e9ab, 901e1f49b24e, c1235c72b2a0].
- **Global Combustion Metrics:** BASS-II data facilitate the calculation of combustion completeness, heat release rates, and global fuel-to-oxygen equivalence ratios, which underpin safety limits and quenching boundaries [4309595ac57d].

---

## Gravity and Mission Applicability
- **Microgravity Relevance:** Findings are directly applicable to spacecraft fire safety, cabin ventilation design, and emergency response protocols on the ISS and future long-duration crewed vehicles [978285f71094, 56b4a5b5e9f0, 9e631276e275]. 
- **Partial-G Applicability:** Experimental observations and methodology provide baseline data and foundational understanding to inform fire growth and suppression design models for lunar and Martian habitats [c140d99f57a3, 3d2e21d44b29, 66b8f4f3d4d3].

---

## Research Gaps and Limitations
- **Model Discrepancies:** Theoretical models consistently predict higher flame spread rates than those observed experimentally in microgravity, indicating that current predictive models require significant improvement [0732e87008c7].
- **Flow and Geometry Variables:** Further quantification of flame dynamics (pulsations, oscillations) and obstructed or wake-stabilized flame behaviors remains necessary to fully resolve suppression limits across diverse mission hardware configurations [56b4a5b5e9f0, c140d99f57a3].

---

## Earth Applications
- The data generated by BASS contribute to fundamental combustion computational models used in the design of fire detection, gaseous inerting, and suppression systems in terrestrial confined spaces and ventilation ducts [978285f71094, 9e631276e275, c140d99f57a3, 3d2e21d44b29, 2cca5c5caaad].
- Results aid researchers in evaluating fuel-burning models for large-scale terrestrial fires, including wildfires, building fires, industrial combustion, energy recapture from waste recycling, and the improvement of flame-retardant clothing and interior textiles [f451aca5e9ab, 3bb14d0c7f4d, 901e1f49b24e, 5873d52278ee].

---

## Key Conclusions
The BASS, BASS-II, and BASS-M investigations demonstrate that solid materials can burn vigorously in microgravity under adequate ventilation, producing distinct oval-shaped flames and specialized spread behaviors dependent on fuel thickness, airflow, and oxygen concentration [978285f71094, 3bb14d0c7f4d, 0cc8af50a6e0]. Because conventional terrestrial fire suppression techniques (such as aiming at the base of a flame) risk exacerbating microgravity fires via oxygen entrainment, evidence-based extinction thresholds and gaseous suppression protocols developed through these spaceflight tests are essential for ensuring crew safety and spacecraft fire protection [56b4a5b5e9f0, f451aca5e9ab].
